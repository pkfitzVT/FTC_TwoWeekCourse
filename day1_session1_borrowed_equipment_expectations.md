# Borrowed Equipment and Community Trust

## This Equipment Comes From a Community

The robots, tools, parts, electronics, batteries, wheels, motors, servos, and building materials used in this course are not just classroom supplies.

Much of this equipment is borrowed from local FIRST Tech Challenge teams. Some materials were purchased through grants and nonprofit support. These materials are being shared with us because other people believe students should have the chance to experience robotics.

That trust makes this course possible.

## Why This Matters

Local robotics teams use these parts for their own competitions, demonstrations, outreach, and learning. When they lend materials, they are trusting us to care for them and return them in good condition whenever possible.

Mr. Fitzgerald has coached FIRST robotics for many years and has built relationships with teams and mentors across the state. Those relationships matter. We do not want to compromise that trust.

Respecting borrowed equipment is part of joining the robotics community.

## Borrowed Equipment Expectations

These expectations apply to every student and every team.

| Expectation | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Treat borrowed parts as community property | These materials are not disposable. They belong to teams, donors, or the course community. |
| Use parts for their intended purpose | Do not misuse tools, electronics, batteries, motors, servos, wheels, or structural parts. |
| Ask before permanently modifying parts | Do not cut, drill, bend, file, glue, tape permanently, or alter borrowed materials without permission. |
| Keep parts organized | Return unused parts to the correct location. Keep goBILDA, REV, Tetrix, electronics, wheels, fasteners, and tools sorted when possible. |
| Report damage honestly | If something breaks, strips, bends, overheats, disappears, or stops working, tell a teacher or mentor right away. |
| Do not hide mistakes | Robotics involves failure and repair. Hiding damage makes the problem worse. |
| Return unused materials quickly | Do not hold parts your team is not actively using. Other teams may need them. |
| Help clean and sort at the end | Returning materials is part of the course, not an optional cleanup task. |

## Limited Materials Are Not First Come, First Served

Some materials will be limited. Not every team will always get the same parts at the same time. That does not mean the process is unfair.

Limited materials will be assigned based on:

- readiness,
- need,
- fairness,
- rotation,
- test plan,
- and return plan.

If your team wants a scarce part, be ready to explain:

## Coopertition Currency

In robotics, teams compete, but they also help each other. This is called **coopertition**.

Coopertition currency means that fairness happens over time. Sometimes your team may receive a limited part, extra help, or extra time with a tool. Later, your team may be asked to share a part, explain a solution, return a resource, or help another team.

The goal is not for every team to have exactly the same materials at every moment. The goal is for every team to have a real chance to learn, build, test, and succeed.

## How to Help Without Taking Over

Helping another team is part of FIRST culture. Taking over another team's work is not.

Good help sounds like:

- "What have you already tried?"
- "Can you show us the problem?"
- "Here is how our team solved something similar."
- "Do you want us to explain it or demonstrate it?"
- "Which part do you want to do yourself?"

Avoid:

- grabbing another team's robot,
- rewriting their code without asking,
- taking their tools or parts,
- mocking their mistake,
- or making decisions for them.

## Returning the Trust

Near the end of the course, teams will help clean, sort, and return borrowed materials. Teams will also write thank-you notes to the people and teams who made this course possible.

That is not extra. It is part of being in the robotics community.
