# Robot Operations Role Plan SLD

## Session / Course Phase

Reusable SLD for performance testing, driving challenges, subsystem tests, practice matches, qualification rounds, and between-round adjustments.

## Why This Discussion Matters

Robot operation is a system.

The robot, code, drivers, operators, coach, pit crew, and communication plan all affect performance.

Competition roles should be based on evidence, fairness, growth, safety, and team needs. The best role plan includes backups.

Roles are not permanent privileges. Roles can be reviewed if safety, reliability, communication, fairness, or team performance require a change.

This SLD is not a popularity vote. It is a decision-making process for assigning roles that help the team perform safely and effectively.

SLD is the decision process. The Robot Operations Role Plan is the work product.

## Main Discussion Question

How should our team assign and practice robot operation roles so we are safe, fair, competitive, and prepared if someone is absent?

Simpler version:

Who should do each robot operation job, who is the backup, and what evidence supports our plan?

## What This Discussion Must Produce

By the end of this SLD, your team should complete a Robot Operations Role Plan that includes:

- primary driver
- backup driver
- primary operator
- backup operator
- coach / spotter, if used
- human operator / loader, if used
- pit repair lead
- software / code lead
- evidence / portfolio recorder
- practice rotation plan
- evidence or reason for role choices
- backup plan if someone is absent
- role review conditions

## Evidence / Resources to Use

Use evidence such as:

- driving trial data
- round-robin challenge results
- controller mapping evidence
- drive power tuning evidence
- penalties
- driver/operator observations
- who communicates clearly
- who stays calm under pressure
- who understands the controls
- who listens to teammates
- who protects the robot
- who improves through practice
- who can explain the robot strategy
- team norms
- safety expectations

The fastest driver is not always the best competition driver.

## Discussion Roles

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | Keeps the team focused on evidence and fairness. |
| Tally keeper | Uses the SLD tally sheet. |
| Recorder | Writes the role plan. |
| Evidence checker | Asks what evidence supports each role choice. |
| Equity checker | Asks whether the plan gives people fair opportunities to practice. |
| Safety checker | Asks whether the role plan protects people, robots, and the field. |

For smaller teams, one student may hold more than one role.

## Role Review / Role Reset

A role review is not punishment. It is a way to protect the robot, the team, and the quality of the work.

A role review may be needed if there are repeated concerns about:

- unsafe operation
- ignoring safety instructions
- reckless contact
- poor communication
- not listening to the operator/coach
- blaming teammates
- refusing to share practice time
- poor sportsmanship
- ignoring the team strategy or role plan
- not preparing a backup
- not improving after feedback

The teacher may immediately pause or change a driving or operating role if safety is a concern.

During a role review, use evidence, avoid personal attacks, and decide whether to:

- keep the role plan
- rotate practice time
- add a backup
- pause a role temporarily
- change a role
- set a practice goal
- ask the teacher for support
