# Session 10 Reading: Target Impact and Shot Consistency

## Reaching the Goal Is Not Enough

A shot can reach the goal and still fail if it hits too hard, hits at a bad angle, or bounces out.

Scoring is about what happens at impact. The ball must not only reach the goal. It must enter the goal in a way that stays in.

## Impact Vocabulary

**Impact** is the moment the ball hits the goal, rim, wall, floor, or another surface.

**Impact angle** is the direction the ball is moving when it hits.

**Impact speed** is how fast the ball is moving when it hits.

A **bounce** happens when the ball hits something and changes direction.

A **rebound** is a bounce back from a surface.

A **skip** is a fast, low bounce that glances off a surface.

A **rim** is the edge of an opening or goal.

A **back wall** is the surface behind or inside a goal that the ball may hit.

An **opening** is the space the ball must pass through to score.

A **cushion** is a feature that helps slow, catch, or redirect the ball.

**Tolerance** is how much error a design can handle and still work.

**Margin of error** is the amount a shot can be off and still score.

**Variation** means the shots are not exactly the same every time.

**Consistency** means getting similar results again and again.

**Repeatability** means the shooter can make the same shot many times under the same conditions.

A **scoring window** is the range of speeds, angles, and positions where the shot still works.

## Impact Angle Matters

A ball dropping downward may settle into the goal more easily. A ball moving mostly forward may hit the back wall or rim and bounce.

This does not mean every shot must be high. It means your team should watch how the ball enters the goal, not just whether it reaches the goal.

## Impact Speed Matters

A very fast shot can skip, bounce, or rebound. A slower shot may be more forgiving, but it might not reach the goal.

Goal shape can provide some cushion. The opening, rim, back wall, or container shape may help redirect some imperfect shots inward.

This cushion is limited. If the ball arrives too fast or at a bad angle, it may still bounce out.

## More Speed Can Create More Variation

Faster shots travel farther, but small changes in angle, compression, or ball position can create bigger differences.

Fast impacts can also make bounce behavior harder to control. Sometimes a slightly slower, more repeatable shot scores more often than a powerful shot.

## What the Shot Behavior Might Mean

| Shot Behavior | What It Might Mean | Possible Team Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Ball falls short | Not enough speed or angle | Increase power or raise angle |
| Ball goes over goal | Too much speed or angle | Lower power or lower angle |
| Ball hits front rim | Path is too low or too short | Increase angle or speed |
| Ball hits back hard and bounces out | Impact speed may be too high or angle too flat | Reduce power or increase arc |
| Ball enters sometimes but not consistently | Variation in compression, ball position, or motor speed | Improve guide rails, compression, or testing routine |

## Design for a Scoring Window

A scoring window is the range of speeds, angles, and positions where the shot still works. A strong design has a larger scoring window, so small mistakes do not ruin the shot.

For example, a shooter with a larger scoring window might still score if the robot is a few inches off or the ball enters the wheel slightly differently.

## Test for Repeatability

Test multiple shots from the same position and record:

- how many shots were attempted,
- how many scored,
- where misses went,
- whether the ball bounced out,
- and whether the shooter changed after repeated shots.
