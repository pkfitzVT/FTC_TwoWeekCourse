# Session 13 Reading: Test Shooting and Observation

## Test Shots Are Evidence

Test shooting is not just "trying it." It is collecting evidence.

The first shots may miss, jam, roll weakly, bounce out, or launch unpredictably. That is normal. Your job is to observe carefully and change one thing at a time.

## Test Shooting Vocabulary

A **test shot** is a shot used to learn something about the robot.

An **observation** is something your team notices during a test.

**Evidence** is information from observations, measurements, or repeated tests.

A **pattern** is something that happens more than once.

**Inconsistent** means the robot does not behave the same way each time.

The **launch angle** is the angle the ball leaves the shooter.

**Launch speed** is how fast the ball leaves the shooter.

**Flywheel recovery** is the time the flywheel needs to return to speed after a shot.

**Compression** is how much the ball is squeezed between the wheel and guide surface.

**Ball contact** is where and how the ball touches the flywheel.

**Impact angle** is the direction the ball is moving when it hits the goal or target.

A **bounce out** happens when the ball enters or hits the goal but comes back out.

An **overshoot** happens when the ball goes over or past the goal.

An **undershoot** happens when the ball falls short.

A **jam** happens when the ball gets stuck.

An **adjustment** is a planned change to the robot or test setup.

A **hypothesis** is your team's best prediction about what change might improve the result.

## Watch the Whole Shot

Do not watch only where the ball lands. Watch the whole system:

- Did the ball enter the flywheel smoothly?
- Did the ball touch the wheel in the same place each time?
- Did the flywheel slow down?
- Did the ball launch too high, too low, too fast, or too slow?
- Did the ball hit the goal, rim, back, or side?
- Did the ball stay in the goal or bounce out?
- Did the hopper feed the next ball too soon?
- Did the trigger reset correctly?

Good observations help your team choose the next adjustment.

## Change One Thing at a Time

Changing many things at once makes it hard to know what fixed the problem.

Examples:

- Change the launch angle, then test.
- Change the flywheel power, then test.
- Change the compression gap, then test.
- Change the trigger timing, then test.
- Change the hopper angle, then test.

## Common First Shot Problems

| Problem | Possible Cause | First Adjustment to Try |
|---|---|---|
| Ball does not enter flywheel | Trigger does not push far enough or ball path is blocked | Check trigger range and clearance |
| Ball jams before flywheel | Hopper opening is too narrow or ball is squeezed | Increase clearance or smooth the guide |
| Ball launches weakly | Flywheel is too slow or compression is too low/high | Check power, wheel contact, and motor recovery |
| Ball overshoots goal | Launch speed is too high or angle is too high | Lower power or adjust angle |
| Ball falls short | Launch speed is too low or angle is too low | Increase power or adjust angle |
| Ball bounces out | Impact speed or angle is not forgiving | Try slower shot or higher arc |
| Next ball enters too soon | Hopper releases more than one ball | Improve one-ball trigger control |
