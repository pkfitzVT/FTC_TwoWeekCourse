# Session 13 Reading: Trigger Implementation and First Shots

## The Trigger Joins the Robot

Today the trigger becomes part of the robot. A trigger that works by hand may not work once it is mounted near the hopper, flywheel, frame, wires, and other parts.

The trigger should control one ball at a time. Before testing with the flywheel running, make sure the trigger moves freely and can return to its starting position.

## Trigger Vocabulary

A **trigger** is the part that releases or pushes a ball into the flywheel.

A **servo** is a small motor unit that can move to a controlled position.

A **servo horn** is the arm attached to the servo output shaft.

A **linkage** is a connected part that transfers servo motion to the trigger.

The **load position** is where the trigger holds the ball back or waits for the next ball.

The **release position** is where the trigger lets one ball enter the flywheel.

**Reset** means returning the trigger to the load position after a shot.

**Clearance** is the space around the trigger, servo horn, ball, frame, and hopper.

**Binding** happens when parts rub, twist, or get stuck so the mechanism cannot move freely.

**Range of motion** means how far the trigger can move.

An **endpoint** is one end of the safe motion range.

**Alignment** means the trigger, hopper, and ball path are lined up so the ball moves where intended.

The **ball path** is the route the ball follows from the hopper into the flywheel.

**One ball at a time** means the trigger releases one ball instead of dumping the whole stack.

A **controlled shot** is a shot where the team knows when and how the ball was released.

A **test shot** is a shot used to collect evidence.

**Reliability** means the trigger works many times without jamming, sticking, or needing constant repair.

## Do Not Force the Servo

The servo should not be forced to push through a jam. If the servo buzzes, strains, hits the frame, or cannot reach the commanded position, stop and adjust the mechanism.

A servo trigger should move a light part. It should not fight the full force of a stuck ball stack.

## Before Running the Flywheel

Test the trigger without launching first:

- Is the servo mounted firmly?
- Is the servo horn attached securely?
- Does the trigger move without hitting the frame?
- Does the trigger have a clear load position?
- Does the trigger have a clear release position?
- Can the trigger return to load position?
- Does the servo buzz or strain?
- Does the ball move as expected when the trigger moves?
- Can the trigger move one ball without releasing the whole stack?

If the answer to any safety question is no, fix that before spinning the flywheel.

## First Controlled Shots

Start with one ball and one shot at a time. The goal is evidence, not rapid firing.

- Load one ball.
- Turn on the flywheel.
- Wait briefly for the flywheel to reach speed.
- Move the trigger to release or push the ball.
- Move the trigger back to load position.
- Stop and observe what happened.
- Record what worked and what failed.

After one controlled shot works, your team can test whether the trigger resets and feeds the next ball.
