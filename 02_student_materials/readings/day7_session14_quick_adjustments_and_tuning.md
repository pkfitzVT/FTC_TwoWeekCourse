# Session 14 Reading: Quick Adjustments and Tuning

## Improving a Robot Quickly

Robots rarely work perfectly after the first build. Quick adjustments are not cheating. They are part of the engineering cycle when used safely and thoughtfully.

Duct tape, zip ties, temporary braces, spacers, cardboard, rubber bands, or small guide pieces can help teams test ideas before building a permanent solution.

Temporary fixes are for learning. They should be replaced with stronger solutions when possible, especially before the final demonstration.

## Tuning Vocabulary

**Tuning** means making small changes to improve reliability or performance.

An **adjustment** is a planned change to a robot part, code value, or operating routine.

A **temporary fix** is a quick change used to test an idea.

A **prototype** is an early version built so the team can learn.

A **refinement** is a small improvement based on evidence.

A **brace** is a support piece that reduces wobble or bending.

A **spacer** is a part that creates a controlled gap between other parts.

A **shim** is a thin spacer used to adjust fit or alignment.

A **guide** is a part that directs a ball, wire, or mechanism along a safer path.

A **zip tie** is a plastic strap often used to hold wires or light parts temporarily.

**Duct tape** is strong tape that can temporarily hold parts or guides during a test.

**Strain relief** means securing a wire so pulling on it does not pull directly on the connector.

To **secure** a part means to hold it firmly so it does not shift during use.

**Loose** means a part can move when it should not.

**Vibration** is shaking that can loosen parts or change alignment.

A **failure mode** is a way the robot can fail, such as a wire rubbing a wheel or a guide falling off.

A **tradeoff** is what you give up when choosing one fix over another.

A **permanent fix** is a stronger, cleaner solution meant to last beyond a quick test.

## Temporary Fixes Are for Learning

A temporary fix helps the team test whether an idea works.

Examples:

- Use a zip tie to keep a wire away from a wheel.
- Use duct tape to hold a guide rail while testing ball path.
- Add a temporary cardboard ramp to test a hopper angle.
- Add a spacer to stop a wheel from rubbing.
- Add a brace to reduce wobble.
- Tape the battery temporarily to test whether shifting weight caused a problem.

If the temporary fix works, your team should plan a stronger version when possible.

## Common Quick Adjustments

| Problem | Quick Test Adjustment | More Permanent Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Battery slides | Tape or strap battery temporarily | Add secure battery mount |
| Ball jams in hopper | Tape in a temporary guide | Build a smoother guide rail |
| Servo hits hopper | Move horn position or limit position value | Remount servo or redesign linkage |
| Wire touches wheel | Zip tie wire away from wheel | Route wire through protected path |
| Shooter angle shifts | Add temporary brace | Build stronger mount |
| Ball feeds too fast | Add temporary gate or stop | Redesign trigger to release one ball |
| Wheel rubs frame | Add spacer or reposition part | Rebuild mount with proper clearance |

## Safety Rules for Quick Fixes

- Do not tape over moving parts that need to move freely.
- Do not put tape or zip ties where they can enter gears, wheels, or the flywheel.
- Do not block the power switch.
- Do not trap wires where they can be cut or pulled.
- Do not force the servo if it is buzzing or stuck.
- Stop the robot before adjusting mechanisms.
- Ask before making a change that affects safety or power.

## Tuning Means Improving Reliability

Tuning is about making the robot more consistent, not just making one lucky shot.

Ask:

- Can it fire one ball?
- Can it fire three balls in a row?
- Can it reload without jamming?
- Does the servo reset each time?
- Does the battery stay secure?
- Do the wires stay clear?
- Does the driver know the timing?
