# Team Norms Menu

## What Kind of Team Do We Want to Become?

## Graduate Expectation Tags

- **[Green: Personal Development]** We identify strengths, make responsible choices, and practice growth.
- **[Blue: Effective Communication]** We express, receive, and respond to ideas.
- **[Orange: Cultural Understanding & Civic Engagement]** We participate responsibly in a team and robotics community.
- **[Red: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving]** We ask questions, think of possible solutions, and make decisions.

## Decision Being Made

Your team is choosing norms that describe how you want to work together. Norms are team habits. They help a group decide how to talk, build, code, test, disagree, recover from mistakes, and include every teammate.

Your team does not need to choose every norm. Choose the norms that best match the kind of team you want to become.

## Background Sources

Use ideas from:

- **Why Engineering Teams Need Different Kinds of People**
- **Borrowed Equipment and Community Trust**
- **Robotics Safety Norms**
- **What Is FIRST?**
- **What Is FIRST Tech Challenge?**
- your individual bios and goals
- the SLD on good robotics teammates

## Why This Decision Matters

Robotics teams work under pressure. Robots break, code fails, parts are limited, and time runs short. A team without clear norms may let one person dominate, leave quiet teammates out, rush unsafe tests, skip documentation, or argue without deciding.

Good norms do not make a team perfect. They give the team a way to return to the work when things get difficult.

## How to Use This Menu

1. Read the options.
2. Discuss what each norm helps and what it might make harder.
3. Choose at least four norms for your team.
4. Record your choices, trade-offs, and evidence plan in the engineering portfolio.
5. Create one repair agreement for what your team will do when a norm breaks.

# Menu Options

## Option A: Everyone Speaks Before Anyone Speaks Twice

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Blue: Effective Communication]

**What it means:**  
At the start of important discussions, every teammate gets a chance to speak before one person speaks again.

**What it helps:**  
This helps quieter teammates enter the conversation and keeps confident speakers from controlling the first version of the decision.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** High if the team is patient

**Time to implement:** Moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
Discussion may feel slower at first. Fast thinkers may need to wait.

**Evidence we might see:**  
More students contribute to SLDs, design choices, and portfolio decisions.

---

## Option B: Ask Before Taking Over

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Green: Personal Development] and [Blue: Effective Communication]

**What it means:**  
If someone is building, coding, driving, or documenting, teammates ask before grabbing the tool, controller, code, or task.

**What it helps:**  
This protects student ownership and helps people learn by doing.

**Ease of use:** Easy

**Likelihood of success:** High if everyone remembers

**Time to implement:** Fast

**Possible trade-off:**  
It may take longer than having the most experienced person do the task.

**Evidence we might see:**  
More students try tools, code, driving, testing, and portfolio tasks.

---

## Option C: Test One Change at a Time

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Red: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving]

**What it means:**  
When debugging or improving the robot, the team changes one thing, tests it, and records what happened before changing something else.

**What it helps:**  
This makes it easier to know what caused an improvement or problem.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** High

**Time to implement:** Moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
It may feel slower when the robot has many problems.

**Evidence we might see:**  
The portfolio has clear test tables showing what changed and what happened.

---

## Option D: Document Before Moving On

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Blue: Effective Communication] and [Red: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving]

**What it means:**  
Before the team leaves a major task, someone adds evidence to the engineering portfolio: photo, sketch, code, data, decision, or next step.

**What it helps:**  
This prevents the team from forgetting decisions and creates evidence for the final presentation.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** High if documentation is assigned

**Time to implement:** Moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
It uses time that could otherwise go to building or driving.

**Evidence we might see:**  
The portfolio is updated every session and includes decisions, test results, and revisions.

---

## Option E: Help Without Taking Over

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Orange: Cultural Understanding & Civic Engagement]

**What it means:**  
When helping another team or teammate, we explain, ask questions, model briefly, or coach. We do not take control of their robot, tools, code, or decision.

**What it helps:**  
This supports coopertition and helps everyone keep ownership of their learning.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** Medium to high

**Time to implement:** Moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
Helping by coaching can take longer than simply doing the task.

**Evidence we might see:**  
Students can describe how they helped another person learn without doing the work for them.

---

## Option F: Name the Trade-Off

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Red: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving]

**What it means:**  
When the team makes a decision, the team states what the choice helps and what it makes harder.

**What it helps:**  
This makes decisions more honest and helps the team avoid pretending one choice is perfect.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** High with practice

**Time to implement:** Fast to moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
It can slow down decisions when the team wants to build immediately.

**Evidence we might see:**  
Decision tables include "why we chose it" and "what we gave up."

---

## Option G: Rotate Roles by Subsystem

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Green: Personal Development]

**What it means:**  
Students try different roles across the course. A person might build during chassis work, help program during servo work, test during shooter work, and present during final preparation.

**What it helps:**  
This gives more students meaningful experience and prevents one person from becoming the only builder, coder, driver, or documenter.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** Medium to high

**Time to implement:** Slow at first, faster with routine

**Possible trade-off:**  
Students may sometimes do a role that is not their favorite or strongest.

**Evidence we might see:**  
The responsibility table shows different students leading different tasks over time.

---

## Option H: Ask for Help After 10 Minutes Stuck

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Green: Personal Development] and [Orange: Cultural Understanding & Civic Engagement]

**What it means:**  
If the team is stuck for about 10 minutes without progress, the team asks a mentor, teacher, or another team for help.

**What it helps:**  
This prevents frustration and wasted time while still giving the team a chance to solve the problem first.

**Ease of use:** Easy

**Likelihood of success:** High

**Time to implement:** Fast

**Possible trade-off:**  
The team has to admit it is stuck, and the helper must avoid taking over.

**Evidence we might see:**  
The team can explain what it tried before asking for help.

---

## Option I: Safety Stops the Test

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Green: Personal Development]

**What it means:**  
Anyone on the team can call "stop" during a robot test if something seems unsafe, stuck, hot, loose, or out of control.

**What it helps:**  
This protects people, robots, tools, and borrowed equipment.

**Ease of use:** Easy

**Likelihood of success:** High

**Time to implement:** Fast

**Possible trade-off:**  
A test may stop before the team gets the data it wanted.

**Evidence we might see:**  
Students stop tests early when needed and record the reason.

---

## Option J: Good Enough to Test

**Graduate Expectation Connection:** [Purple: Curiosity & Creativity] and [Red: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving]

**What it means:**  
The team decides when an idea is ready for a first test, even if it is not perfect.

**What it helps:**  
This prevents over-planning and helps the team learn from real evidence.

**Ease of use:** Medium

**Likelihood of success:** Medium

**Time to implement:** Moderate

**Possible trade-off:**  
The first version may fail, look rough, or need rebuilding.

**Evidence we might see:**  
The team builds prototypes, tests them, records what happened, and revises.
