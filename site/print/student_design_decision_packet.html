Demo Robot Design Analysis

Students should complete this form while operating, observing, or testing a demonstration robot.

The purpose is to collect evidence about different robot designs before making team design decisions. Use these notes later during strategy, robot behavior, chassis, flywheel, hopper, and trigger discussions.

Demo robot analysis creates design evidence. Students use that evidence during strategy, robot behavior, and chassis design discussions.

This activity works best across two sessions. Complete one form for each demo robot you analyze. If there are four demo robots, try to collect evidence from all four before your team finalizes major design choices.

Students should operate demo robots with more than one partner when possible. The goal is to experience how communication, timing, and role clarity affect robot operation. This is not a ranking activity. It is a chance to practice working with different people and to notice what helps a drive team function well.

Rotation Directions

  • Work in pairs when possible.
  • Each robot should have a driver and an operator or observer.
  • If two pairs are assigned to one robot, take turns operating and recording.
  • Record evidence from what you did and what you observed.
  • Rotate partners when possible so you experience different communication styles.
  • Focus on what the design makes easier or harder. Do not rank classmates or drivers.

Demo Robot Design Observation Activity

Purpose

Use demonstration robots as physical examples of design choices.

Demo robots are not perfect answers. They are examples you can inspect, question, copy, modify, or avoid.

Some demo robots may later be disassembled and used for parts. Your photos, sketches, and notes matter because they preserve the design ideas your team noticed.

Big Idea

Student-led discussion helps the team make a decision, but the deliverable is the design choice the team records, builds toward, tests, and revises.

Design menus are not extra worksheets. Design menus help teams turn observations and discussion into build targets.

What To Do

1. Walk around the demo robots with your team. 2. Take photos or make sketches of useful design details. 3. Name the subsystem you are observing. 4. Use design menu language to describe the choice you noticed. 5. Decide whether your team might copy, modify, or avoid that idea. 6. Add the strongest photo/sketch and design note to your engineering notebook.

Observation Prompts

Frame / Drivetrain

  • What chassis or frame shape do you see?
  • Does it look like an O-frame, U-frame, A-frame, H-frame, or hybrid?
  • Where are the drive motors placed?
  • Where are the wheels placed?
  • What space is open or blocked?
  • What looks easy to repair?
  • What looks hard to assemble?

Shooter

  • Where could a shooter fit on this robot?
  • If a shooter is already mounted, how is it supported?
  • What angle or launch path does the design suggest?
  • What parts might flex, shake, or move during a shot?
  • What would make the shooter easier or harder to adjust?

Hopper / Artifact Management

  • How are artifacts held?
  • How are artifacts guided?
  • Does gravity help or hurt this design?
  • Where might artifacts jam?
  • How does the hopper connect to the shooter or release mechanism?
  • How would a human operator reload this robot?

Trigger / Release

  • How are artifacts released?
  • Does the trigger look like it releases one artifact at a time or a batch?
  • Where might a servo, linkage, or gate fit?
  • What safe start position would this mechanism need?
  • What could block reset or jam recovery?

Whole Robot / Integration

  • What design choice might create problems later?
  • What subsystem seems to control the most space?
  • Where are wires, battery, and Control Hub located?
  • What would be hard to reach during repair?
  • What would your team copy, change, or avoid?

Use this guide after your team has formed, chosen a team name, and selected team norms. Copy it into your engineering portfolio or shared team document, then revisit it before major design decisions.

The behavior specification turns your strategy into specific robot actions, performance goals, hardware needs, code needs, and tests.

Core idea:

Team norms tell us how we will work together.
Game strategy tells us what we are trying to accomplish.
Robot design turns that strategy into a physical system.

Before making a major robot decision, ask:

What scoring strategy does this support?

Before choosing chassis geometry, frame material, motor placement, or mechanism design, ask:

Which robot behavior does this design choice support?

Before adding complexity, ask:

Will this improve our expected score enough to be worth the risk?

What scoring strategy does this support?

Before adding complexity, ask:

Will this improve our expected score enough to be worth the risk?

Before changing the plan, ask:

  • What evidence changed our thinking?
  • Does everyone understand the new strategy?
  • What design, code, driver practice, or documentation must change next?
  • Is this change consistent with our team norms?

Use this template after your team has a first strategy and before choosing major robot design details.

Example:

Behavior specification: Our robot must drive from the starting area to the scoring position, align with the goal, release or shoot 2 artifacts per scoring loop, return for more artifacts, avoid contact, and fully park with 15 seconds remaining.

Controller Mapping Drive Comparison Activity

Purpose

Use evidence to decide how your team should control the frame/drivetrain subsystem.

You will compare two drive-only OpModes:

  • DemoDriveDPad.java: D-pad and bumper driving with helper methods.
  • DemoDriveJoy.java: joystick arcade driving with direct motor-power math.

Use the OnBot Java code examples README before copying either file.

This is a controller-mapping decision. Your choice should support your Robot Behavior Specification, your Subsystem Design Cycle evidence, and your team strategy.

Before You Start

Make sure your team has:

  • a frame/drivetrain subsystem that is mechanically safe to test,
  • Robot Controller configuration names leftDrive and rightDrive,
  • wheels lifted for the first powered test,
  • a test area with clear boundaries,
  • one driver, one safety watcher, and one recorder.

Do not add shooter, hopper, trigger, or servo code during this activity. Test the drivetrain subsystem by itself first.

Step 1: Run DemoDriveDPad

Copy DemoDriveDPad.java into OnBot Java, build it, and run it safely.

Record what happens when the driver uses:

  • D-pad up
  • D-pad down
  • left bumper
  • right bumper

Step 2: Run DemoDriveJoy

Copy DemoDriveJoy.java into OnBot Java, build it, and run it safely.

Record what happens when the driver uses:

  • left stick Y for forward/back
  • right stick X for turning

Code Organization Challenge

DemoDriveDPad uses helper methods such as driveForward() and allStop().

DemoDriveJoy puts the joystick math directly inside the main loop.

Challenge: rewrite DemoDriveJoy using helper methods so the main loop is easier to read.

Possible helper methods:

  • driveWithJoystick()
  • setDrivePower(leftPower, rightPower)
  • clipPower(power)
  • showDriveTelemetry(drive, turn, leftPower, rightPower)
