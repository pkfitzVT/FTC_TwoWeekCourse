What Should I Be Doing Right Now?

Robotics Work-Ahead Menu

In robotics, not everyone can physically build on the robot at the same time. That does not mean the rest of the team is waiting.

A strong engineering team works in parallel. While one or two students are building, other students can move the robot forward through programming, testing, design, documentation, driving practice, or organization.

Idle time is engineering time.
If you are not building, you should be helping the team prepare, test, improve, code, document, or explain the robot.

---

The First Question

Before choosing a task, ask:

What does the robot need next?

Then choose the highest-value task you can do from the menu below.

---

Priority Menu for Productive Work

Priority 1: Programming / Controls

Programming is one of the best ways to work ahead because code can often be prepared before the robot is fully ready.

Choose a programming task if the team needs the robot to move, respond to a controller, operate a mechanism, or display useful information.

Useful programming tasks

- startShooter() - stopShooter() - fireTrigger() - openGate() - closeGate()

  • Open and read the current TeleOp.
  • Check that motor and servo names match the robot configuration.
  • Add comments explaining what the code does.
  • Create or update a gamepad control map.
  • Add gamepad2 controls for a mechanism.
  • Add telemetry to show motor power, servo position, or sensor readings.
  • Test one motor or servo at a time.
  • Prepare safe starting positions for servos.

  • Debug one control problem at a time.

Ask yourself

What should the robot do when the driver or operator presses a button, joystick, or trigger?

---

Priority 2: Testing / Data

Robots should not be improved by guessing. They should be improved using evidence.

Choose a testing or data task if the team needs to know whether the robot is actually getting better.

Useful testing tasks

  • Measure how long the robot takes to drive a set distance.
  • Measure how consistently the robot drives straight.
  • Time a turn or a driver-control task.
  • Count successful shots out of 10 trials.
  • Measure launch distance.
  • Measure launch angle.
  • Record whether a mechanism jams.
  • Compare performance before and after a design change.
  • Identify the most common failure.
  • Make a simple table of results.
  • Create a graph of speed, distance, success rate, or launch angle.

Example test table

Trial What we tested Result Notes
1 6-foot drive ___ seconds
2 6-foot drive ___ seconds
3 6-foot drive ___ seconds

Ask yourself

What can we measure that would help the team make a better design decision?

---

Priority 3: Design, Measurement, and Templates

Design work does not have to happen in professional CAD software. Design can happen on paper, cardboard, whiteboards, or a Chromebook.

The important engineering skill is turning an idea into a measured, buildable part.

Useful design tasks

  • Sketch the current robot or mechanism.
  • Draw a side view or top view.
  • Label important parts.
  • Measure hole spacing, axle height, or mounting distance.
  • Make a paper or cardboard template.
  • Hold the template against the robot to check fit.
  • Mark where holes, slots, motors, servos, or brackets should go.
  • Identify where the part might collide with something else.
  • Compare two design options.
  • Revise the sketch after looking at the actual robot.
  • Try a simple browser-based CAD drawing if available and useful.

Ask yourself

What can we measure, sketch, template, or design that helps the robot work better?

---

Priority 4: Documentation / Engineering Portfolio

A strong engineering team keeps evidence of its thinking. Documentation is not something to do at the end. It should happen throughout the build.

Useful documentation tasks

  • Take clear photos of the robot.
  • Take photos before and after a major change.
  • Record the team’s design decision.
  • Explain why the team changed the design.
  • Save or copy important code snippets.
  • Record test data.
  • Write down what failed and what the team learned.
  • Update the part list.
  • Update the team’s goal or next step.
  • Prepare evidence for the final presentation.

Ask yourself

If someone looked at our portfolio, would they understand our engineering story?

---

Priority 5: Driving / Operations Practice

A robot is only useful if the team can operate it. Driving and operator practice are real engineering work.

Useful driving and operations tasks

  • Practice smooth driving.
  • Practice turning accurately.
  • Practice lining up with a target.
  • Practice driver/operator communication.
  • Create a short demo routine.
  • Time how long a task takes.
  • Notice what feels difficult for the driver.
  • Suggest design or code changes that would make the robot easier to control.

Ask yourself

What does the robot feel like from the driver’s point of view?

---

Priority 6: Tools, Inventory, and Workflow

Good teams work faster because their materials are organized and ready.

Useful organization tasks

  • Organize parts.
  • Return unused parts.
  • Find needed fasteners.
  • Check battery charge.
  • Make sure tools are in the correct place.
  • Label wires or components.
  • Check that wires are not being pulled, pinched, or damaged.
  • Help the team avoid losing small parts.
  • Prepare the next set of parts the builders will need.

Ask yourself

What can I do to make the team faster, safer, or more organized?

---

If You Feel Stuck

Do not say, “I have nothing to do.”

Instead, ask one of these questions:

  • What is the next checkpoint?
  • What part of the robot currently works?
  • What part does not work yet?
  • What code needs to be ready next?
  • What evidence are we missing?
  • What needs to be documented?
  • What can we test while the builders are working?
  • What can we measure?
  • What can we sketch or template?
  • What can we organize so the next step goes faster?

---

Team Expectation

Every team member should be able to answer:

What am I doing right now that helps the team move forward?

You do not need to be building every minute.

You do need to be contributing every minute.
