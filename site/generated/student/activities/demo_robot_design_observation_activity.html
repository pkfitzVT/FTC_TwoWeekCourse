Demo Robot Design Observation Activity

Purpose

Use demonstration robots as physical examples of design choices.

Demo robots are not perfect answers. They are examples you can inspect, question, copy, modify, or avoid.

Some demo robots may later be disassembled and used for parts. Your photos, sketches, and notes matter because they preserve the design ideas your team noticed.

Big Idea

Student-led discussion helps the team make a decision, but the deliverable is the design choice the team records, builds toward, tests, and revises.

Design menus are not extra worksheets. Design menus help teams turn observations and discussion into build targets.

What To Do

1. Walk around the demo robots with your team. 2. Take photos or make sketches of useful design details. 3. Name the subsystem you are observing. 4. Use design menu language to describe the choice you noticed. 5. Decide whether your team might copy, modify, or avoid that idea. 6. Add the strongest photo/sketch and design note to your engineering notebook.

Observation Prompts

Frame / Drivetrain

  • What chassis or frame shape do you see?
  • Does it look like an O-frame, U-frame, A-frame, H-frame, or hybrid?
  • Where are the drive motors placed?
  • Where are the wheels placed?
  • What space is open or blocked?
  • What looks easy to repair?
  • What looks hard to assemble?

Shooter

  • Where could a shooter fit on this robot?
  • If a shooter is already mounted, how is it supported?
  • What angle or launch path does the design suggest?
  • What parts might flex, shake, or move during a shot?
  • What would make the shooter easier or harder to adjust?

Hopper / Artifact Management

  • How are artifacts held?
  • How are artifacts guided?
  • Does gravity help or hurt this design?
  • Where might artifacts jam?
  • How does the hopper connect to the shooter or release mechanism?
  • How would a human operator reload this robot?

Trigger / Release

  • How are artifacts released?
  • Does the trigger look like it releases one artifact at a time or a batch?
  • Where might a servo, linkage, or gate fit?
  • What safe start position would this mechanism need?
  • What could block reset or jam recovery?

Whole Robot / Integration

  • What design choice might create problems later?
  • What subsystem seems to control the most space?
  • Where are wires, battery, and Control Hub located?
  • What would be hard to reach during repair?
  • What would your team copy, change, or avoid?
