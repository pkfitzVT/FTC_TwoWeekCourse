Robotics Safety Norms

Safety Comes Before the Robot

In this course, you will work with real robotics equipment: metal parts, fasteners, motors, servos, wheels, batteries, wires, controllers, tools, and moving mechanisms. These materials are exciting to use, but they also require attention and care.

No robot test, build task, or match practice is more important than safety.

Core Safety Expectations

These expectations apply to every student, every team, and every session.

Safety Norm What It Means
Use tools correctly Use each tool only for its intended purpose. Ask before using a tool you have not been shown how to use.
Wear safety glasses when required Safety glasses are required when cutting, drilling, filing, or working near moving mechanisms.
Keep the test area clear Robots should only drive or test mechanisms when people, backpacks, chairs, and loose parts are out of the way.
Announce before testing Before powering on, pressing INIT, pressing PLAY, or testing a moving mechanism, the team must clearly announce the test.
Keep hands and clothing away from moving parts Hair, sleeves, hoodie strings, hands, and loose objects must stay away from wheels, gears, motors, flywheels, belts, and servos.
Start slowly First tests should use low power or careful movement whenever possible.
Stop when something seems wrong Stop immediately if something binds, slips, overheats, smokes, sparks, sounds wrong, or moves unexpectedly.
Do not bypass safety systems Do not bypass switches, fuses, battery connections, or adult instructions.
Report problems honestly Broken parts, stripped screws, loose wires, damaged tools, or unsafe behavior should be reported right away.

Robot Testing Norms

Before a robot test, the team should be able to answer:

  • What are we testing?
  • What do we expect to happen?
  • Who is operating the Driver Station?
  • Who is watching the robot?
  • Who is recording what happens?
  • What should make us stop the test?

During a test:

  • One person operates the controller.
  • One person watches the robot or mechanism.
  • One person records results.
  • Everyone else stays clear of the robot and field area.
  • Anyone may call "stop" if something seems unsafe.

After a test:

  • Record what happened.
  • Decide whether the problem was mechanical, electrical, software, driver control, or design.
  • Make one change at a time when possible.
  • Update the engineering portfolio with evidence.

Tool and Fastener Norms

Robotics parts often use small screws, nuts, bolts, hubs, set screws, brackets, and hex wrenches. Small mistakes can cause big problems.

Norm Why It Matters
Use the correct hex wrench The wrong tool can strip a screw and make the part hard to remove.
Do not force parts together If holes do not line up, stop and ask for help or redesign the connection.
Keep small parts organized Lost screws, nuts, hubs, and spacers slow down the whole group.
Return tools after use Shared tools need to be available for all teams.
Do not leave tools on the floor or field Tools on the floor can be stepped on, lost, or run over by robots.

Safety and FIRST Culture

FIRST robotics is built around teamwork, professionalism, and respect. Acting safely is part of being a good teammate. It shows that you respect your team, other teams, the equipment, and the people who made this course possible.
