Session 11 Reading: Hopper Jams and Ball Path Testing

Test Before Adding the Servo

A hopper should be tested before your team adds a servo trigger. The first version of a hopper often jams or feeds inconsistently. That is normal engineering work.

Your job is to watch the ball path, find the trouble spots, and revise the design using evidence.

Hopper Troubleshooting Vocabulary

A jam happens when a ball gets stuck and stops feeding.

A bottleneck is a narrow place where balls slow down or stop.

A pinch point is a place where the ball gets squeezed between parts.

A ball stack is a group of balls resting on each other in the hopper.

Feed rate means how quickly balls move toward the shooter.

One-at-a-time feeding means only one ball reaches the flywheel or trigger at a time.

A contact point is a place where the ball touches the hopper, ramp, rail, or trigger.

A guide is a part that directs the ball along the intended path.

Ramp angle is how steep the ramp is.

Friction is rubbing force. Too much friction can stop the ball from rolling or sliding.

Tolerance is how much small error the design can handle and still work.

A test is a careful check to see what happens.

Evidence is information your team can observe, record, or measure.

To redesign means to change the design after learning from a test.

A failure point is the place most likely to jam, bend, loosen, or stop working.

Common Hopper Problems

Common problems include:

  • balls stack too tightly,
  • balls bridge across the opening and stop moving,
  • the slope is too shallow, so gravity does not move the ball,
  • the slope is too steep, so the ball moves too fast,
  • the opening is too narrow,
  • the opening is too wide and lets multiple balls through,
  • the ball rubs against the sides,
  • a screw, bracket, or edge catches the ball,
  • and the bottom ball cannot separate from the stack.

If the hopper jams, do not just shake the robot and move on. Find where and why the jam happened.

Test the Ball Path by Hand

Before adding a servo, place balls in the hopper and watch what happens. Move slowly. You are testing the shape of the path, not trying to score yet.

Check:

  • Does the ball roll or slide toward the trigger?
  • Does the same ball path happen more than once?
  • Does the bottom ball separate from the stack?
  • Does the ball stop or jam anywhere?
  • Does the ball enter the flywheel area in the same position each time?
  • Can a human operator load balls easily?
  • Can the team clear a jam quickly?
