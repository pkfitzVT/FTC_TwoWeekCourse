Session 16 Reading: Awards, Judging, and Growth Narrative

What Judges Look For

Judges are interested in the robot, but also in the team's process, learning, communication, and growth.

Your team should identify which awards or recognitions you believe you have earned and prepare evidence to support those claims.

The categories below are course-friendly recognition categories, not official FTC award names.

Judging Vocabulary

Award criteria are the qualities judges look for when deciding recognition.

Evidence is proof that supports what your team says.

Judging is the process of listening, asking questions, and reviewing evidence.

A claim is something your team says is true.

Support is the evidence that backs up a claim.

Documentation is the record of sketches, photos, notes, code, tests, and decisions.

An engineering notebook is where the team records decisions, tests, changes, and learning.

A design rationale is the reason behind a design choice.

Innovation means a creative or original solution to a problem.

Control means how code, driver input, or programmed behavior helps the robot work.

Reliability means the robot works repeatedly without constant repair.

Perseverance means continuing through a hard problem instead of giving up.

Collaboration means working together and sharing responsibility.

Team dynamic is how the team communicates, solves conflict, and includes people.

A growth narrative is a story about how the team changed and improved over time.

Impact is the effect the team or robot had on learning, confidence, or the group.

Reflection is thinking carefully about what happened and what it means.

Course-Friendly Recognition Categories

  • Design Award: thoughtful robot design and clear explanation of design choices.
  • Innovation Award: creative solution to a problem.
  • Control Award: effective use of code, sensors, or programmed control.
  • Build Quality Award: strong, reliable construction.
  • Teamwork Award: strong collaboration and role sharing.
  • Engineering Process Award: strong testing, documentation, and iteration.
  • Perseverance Award: team overcame a major challenge.
  • Communication Award: clear, engaging presentation.

Make a Claim and Support It

Do not just say, "We deserve the Design Award." Say what you did and show evidence.

Example:

Claim: We should be considered for the Engineering Process Award.
Evidence: Our first hopper jammed because two balls entered the trigger at the same time. We tested three different guide angles and recorded which one fed one ball most consistently.

Growth Narrative

A growth narrative tells how the team changed over time.

Think about:

  • How did the team work together at first?
  • What was hard about communication?
  • How did the team divide roles?
  • What conflict or confusion happened?
  • How did the team solve problems?
  • What did the team learn about engineering?
  • What did the team learn about working with people?

Prepare for Judge Questions

Possible questions:

  • What was your biggest design challenge?
  • What failed first?
  • What did you change after testing?
  • How did your team decide between two designs?
  • How did you make sure the robot was safe?
  • What did each team member contribute?
  • What would you improve with one more week?
  • Which award do you think your team earned, and what evidence supports that?
