What Is FIRST Tech Challenge?

Design. Build. Code. Compete. Explain. Improve.

FIRST Tech Challenge, or FTC, is a robotics program for students in grades 7-12. Each year, FTC teams receive a new game challenge. Teams design, build, program, and drive robots to complete tasks on a field.

FTC robots are smaller than the large robots used in FIRST Robotics Competition, but FTC is still a real engineering challenge. Teams must think about strategy, hardware, software, driving, testing, documentation, and presentation.

In FTC, students work with mentors, but the team does the thinking and learning. A strong FTC team needs many different kinds of people.

Team Role What That Person Might Do
Designer Sketches ideas, plans robot systems, thinks about trade-offs
Builder / Fabricator Uses parts, tools, fasteners, brackets, wheels, motors, and structure
Programmer Writes code for TeleOp, autonomous, sensors, motors, and servos
Driver / Operator Controls the robot during matches and helps test performance
Tester / Data Lead Collects evidence, times trials, records scores, studies reliability
Portfolio Lead Documents design decisions, photos, code, testing, and reflections
Presenter Explains the robot, the team, the process, and the evidence
Outreach / Stewardship Lead Helps the team share, thank, organize, and support the community

FTC is large and international. During the 2024-2025 season, more than 109,000 students in 81 countries participated in FIRST Tech Challenge.

FTC is also designed to be more manageable than some larger robotics programs. FIRST says a team can start FTC for around $1,500, including registration and an initial robotics set investment. Parts can often be reused from year to year, and teams can grow over time.

FTC has a flexible season. Teams do not all need to build the same robot. Different teams can choose different strategies, mechanisms, and levels of complexity. A beginner team might focus on a simple, reliable robot. An advanced team might use sensors, cameras, mecanum wheels, complex mechanisms, or advanced autonomous code.

In this course, we are building an FTC-style experience in two weeks. That means we will simplify some things on purpose. A full competition team might build a more advanced robot, but our goal is for every team to:

  • build a working robot,
  • drive and test it,
  • program basic TeleOp and autonomous actions,
  • document design decisions,
  • collect evidence,
  • explain trade-offs,
  • and present what they learned.

FTC is a competition, but it is also a community. Teams compete, but they also help each other. This is called coopertition: competing seriously while still supporting other teams.

Big Idea

FTC gives students many ways to participate. You do not have to start as an expert. You can contribute through building, coding, driving, testing, documenting, presenting, strategy, creativity, or teamwork.
