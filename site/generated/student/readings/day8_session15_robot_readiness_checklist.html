Session 15 Reading: Robot Readiness Checklist

Check Before You Demonstrate

Teams should inspect the robot before running matches or demonstrations. The robot must be safe, legal-sized, powered correctly, and able to start and drive.

A readiness check helps your team find issues while there is still time to fix them.

Readiness Vocabulary

Readiness means the robot and team are prepared to run safely and reliably.

A checklist is a list used to make sure important items are checked.

Pass means the item is acceptable right now.

To revise means to change something based on what the team found.

To retest means to check again after a fix.

To secure a part means to attach it so it does not move unexpectedly.

Accessible means easy to reach, such as a power switch or battery connector.

Loose means a part can move when it should not.

Mounted means attached to the robot.

To route wires means to place wires along a safe path away from moving parts.

A short circuit is an unsafe electrical path that can damage parts or create heat.

A drive test is a controlled test to confirm the robot can move and stop.

The start position is how the robot begins the match or demonstration.

An emergency stop is a fast way to stop the robot when something is unsafe.

A field inspection is a check before the robot runs on the field or demonstration area.

Robot Size Check

  • Robot must fit inside the 18-inch cube or course size constraint.
  • Check the robot in its starting configuration.
  • Make sure nothing sticks out unexpectedly.
  • If a mechanism moves, make sure the starting position still fits.

Safety Check

  • No sharp edges.
  • No exposed points or cutting hazards.
  • No pinch points where fingers are likely to go.
  • Wheels, flywheel, and moving parts are guarded or treated carefully.
  • Screws and brackets are secure.
  • Nothing is loose enough to fall off during driving.
  • Students know where not to put hands while the robot is running.

Wiring and Power Check

  • Battery is secure.
  • Power switch is working.
  • Power switch is easy to reach.
  • Wires are not touching wheels, gears, flywheel, or moving parts.
  • Wires have strain relief where possible.
  • Motor wires are fully connected.
  • Servo wires are fully connected.
  • No wire is pinched under metal or wood.
  • Control Hub is mounted securely.
  • Battery is charged.

Robot Function Check

  • Robot turns on.
  • Driver Station connects.
  • Correct OpMode appears.
  • Robot initializes without error.
  • Robot starts and stops correctly.
  • Robot can drive forward and backward.
  • Robot can turn.
  • Flywheel turns on and off.
  • Servo trigger moves to load and release positions.
  • Hopper feeds one ball at a time.
  • Robot can complete a basic load-fire-reset sequence.
