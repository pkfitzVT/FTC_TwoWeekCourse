SLD 1: Shared Materials, Borrowed Equipment, and Trust

SLD Type

Community + Synthesis SLD

This discussion helps us connect FIRST values, FTC team culture, safety expectations, and borrowed equipment into one idea: robotics depends on trust.

Why This Discussion Matters

In this course, we will use real robotics equipment. Some of it is borrowed from local FTC teams. Some of it was purchased through grants and nonprofit support. These materials are not disposable classroom supplies. They are part of a larger robotics community.

In robotics, trust is a real resource. Teams lend parts, share tools, explain solutions, and help each other repair robots. If teams do not return materials, report damage honestly, or treat equipment carefully, that trust breaks down. If teams act responsibly, the whole robotics community becomes stronger.

Background Sources

Use ideas from:

  • What Is FIRST?
  • What Is FIRST Tech Challenge?
  • Robotics Safety Norms
  • Borrowed Equipment and Community Trust
  • FIRST / FTC orientation videos
  • Your first observations of the robots, tools, parts, and driving setup

Vocabulary Review

Term Meaning
Coopertition Competing seriously while also helping other teams improve
Gracious professionalism Working hard, respecting others, and acting with integrity
Stewardship Taking care of something that belongs to a community
Trust Confidence that people will act responsibly
Borrowed materials Equipment we use but must return in good condition whenever possible
Shared materials Materials used by the whole course, not owned by one team

Main Discussion Prompt

What do we owe to the teams, mentors, and community members who made this robotics equipment available to us?

Supporting Questions

Use these if the discussion slows down:

  • Why does trust matter in robotics?
  • What could damage trust between teams?
  • How is borrowed robotics equipment different from regular classroom supplies?
  • What should happen if something breaks, strips, bends, overheats, or disappears?
  • How can teams compete while still sharing materials?
  • Why should limited materials not simply be first come, first served?

Discussion Moves

Strong discussions use different kinds of contributions. You do not need to use every move in every discussion. The goal is to help the group think better.

Code Discussion Move What It Sounds Like
Q Question "One question I have is..."
S Source / Quote "The handout says..."
O Observation / Experience "When I watched, drove, tested, or saw..., I noticed..."
C Claim / Reasoning "I think ___ because..."
B Build on Peer "I want to build on what ___ said..."
T Trade-Off "One benefit is..., but one risk is..."
E Evidence / Example "Evidence for that is..."
N Next Step / Decision "This means we should..."

Must / Should / Could Participation

Level What Participation Looks Like
Must Do I listen actively and make at least one contribution using a source, observation, claim, or question.
Should Do I explain my reasoning, connect to evidence, respond to a peer, or name a trade-off.
Could Do I help move the discussion forward by building on a peer, asking a deeper question, synthesizing multiple ideas, inviting another voice, or suggesting a next step.
