Game Strategy SLD

Session / Course Phase

Day 2, Session 4: Strategy before major robot design choices

Why This Discussion Matters

Before your team chooses chassis shape, wheels, shooter design, hopper design, trigger design, controls, or autonomous goals, the team needs a practical game plan. A strategy is not a wish list. It is the plan your team will build, test, practice, revise, and explain.

SLD is the process. The deliverable is the work product that comes out of the discussion.

Strategy / Behavior / Design Framing

Strategy chooses the game plan.
Robot behaviors describe what the robot must do.
Subsystem designs explain how the robot will do it.

Main Discussion Question

What strategy gives our team the best chance to score reliably, avoid penalties, and complete a working robot in the time available?

What This Discussion Must Produce

By the end of this SLD, complete or update the Team Strategy Guide with:

  • primary scoring strategy
  • backup strategy
  • autonomous priority
  • TeleOp priority
  • endgame / parking priority
  • risk level
  • expected score estimate
  • tradeoffs accepted
  • what design choices this strategy will influence

Evidence / Resources to Use

  • Modified game rules and scoring guide
  • Game strategy goals menu
  • Structure/function reading
  • Demo Robot Design Analysis notes about reliability, difficulty, driver/operator roles, and design features
  • Team norms and team identity
  • Challenge by Choice expectations
  • Available materials
  • Competition video observations, if available
  • SLD tally sheet
  • Team Strategy Guide template

Discussion Roles

Role Job
Facilitator Keeps the team focused on strategy, not random robot features.
Tally keeper Uses the SLD tally sheet.
Recorder Updates the Team Strategy Guide.
Reality checker Asks whether the strategy is buildable and testable in the time available.
Equity checker, if needed Watches whether all voices are included.

Sentence Stems

  • Our strongest scoring opportunity might be ___ because ___.
  • A reliable strategy for our team would be ___ because ___.
  • This strategy fits our time and materials because ___.
  • One risk in this strategy is ___.
  • A backup strategy could be ___.
  • This would affect our chassis design because ___.
  • This would affect our shooter/hopper/trigger design because ___.
  • This strategy should guide later design choices because ___.
  • I could support this strategy if ___.
  • Before we commit, we need to check ___.
