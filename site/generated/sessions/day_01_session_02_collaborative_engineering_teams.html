Day 1, Session 2: Collaborative Engineering Teams

Purpose

Establish a course culture where students participate fully, rotate through different kinds of work, and help each other try unfamiliar robotics tasks.

Learning Target

Students can describe how they might contribute to an engineering team and how teammates can make room for different strengths.

Success Criteria

  • Students identify several useful team contributions.
  • Students distinguish helping from taking over.
  • Students name one behavior that invites participation.

Teacher Preparation

Prepare the collaborative engineering teams reading and decide whether student bio information will support team formation.

Student Background Reading / Preparation

  • Collaborative engineering teams
  • Student bio template

Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: How should an engineering team make room for different kinds of contributors?

Work Time Options

  • Discussion: strengths, quiet voices, and helping without taking over.
  • Documentation: individual contribution note.
  • Team formation: create or confirm teams of 4-5 students.

Must / Should / Could

  • Must: Record one contribution the student can make.
  • Should: Identify one kind of work the student wants to try.
  • Could: Name a way to help a teammate learn without taking over.

Deliverables

  • Teamwork reflection or bio-based contribution note.
  • Optional team formation notes.
  • Engineering portfolio entry.

Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Students should add one contribution they can make, one kind of work they want to try, and one team behavior they want to practice.

Reflection / Share-Out

  • What is one kind of work you want to try during the course?
  • What does helpful teamwork sound like?

Teacher Notes

Notice leadership patterns and students who may need structured entry points. Reinforce that students are not locked into fixed roles.
