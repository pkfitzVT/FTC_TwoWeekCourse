Day 2, Session 3: Team Identity and Team Norms

Purpose

Give teams two low-risk decision-making experiences before major robot design choices: choose team norms first, then choose a team identity/name shaped by those norms. Connect both choices to the first game strategy document.

Learning Target

Students can help choose team norms and a team identity/name using reasons, trade-offs, and discussion evidence.

Success Criteria

  • Each team chooses norms and explains why they matter.
  • Each team chooses a name connected to those norms.
  • Teams record an all-voices plan, disagreement plan, and reset/repair plan.
  • Teams record trade-offs and repair agreements.
  • Teams begin a Team Strategy Guide and preview the Robot Behavior Specification that they will revisit before major design discussions.

Teacher Preparation

Prepare team assignments, the Team Norms SLD, the Team Identity / Team Name SLD, the team name menu, the team norms menu, the SLD tally sheet, the Team Strategy Guide template, the Robot Behavior Specification template, Demo Robot Design Analysis notes from Sessions 1-2, and portfolio recording space. If demo robots are available, stage them for brief observation before teams finalize early design ideas.

Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Run the team formation discussion as two short SLDs:

1. Team Norms SLD: What norms will help our team include everyone, make decisions fairly, and keep a working robot moving forward? 2. Team Identity / Team Name SLD: What kind of team do we want to become, and what name represents that identity?

Work Time Options

  • Discussion: team norms before team identity/name.
  • Design: team identity statement, name, possible logo, motto, or color ideas.
  • Documentation: norms work product and identity/name work product.
  • Team process: all-voices plan, disagreement plan, and repair agreement for when norms are not followed.
  • Reading/discussion: connect structure and function to the idea that robot form should follow game strategy.
  • Strategy: copy the Team Strategy Guide into the team portfolio or shared document and complete the identity, norms connection, and first game-understanding sections.
  • Requirements: preview the Robot Behavior Specification so students see how strategy becomes robot actions and performance goals before design choices.
  • Observation: review Demo Robot Design Analysis notes, inspect demo robots if available, take photos or sketches, and name design choices that might support or conflict with the team's strategy.

Must / Should / Could

  • Must: Choose 3-5 team norms, choose a team identity/name connected to those norms, and start a Team Strategy Guide.
  • Should: Explain the trade-off for each norm, create an all-voices plan, disagreement plan, and repair agreement, and preview the Robot Behavior Specification.
  • Could: Watch or assign competition video observation before finalizing the first strategy.

Deliverables

  • SLD document.
  • Team norms work product.
  • Team identity/name work product.
  • Team agreement.
  • Started Team Strategy Guide copied into the team portfolio or shared document.
  • Robot Behavior Specification location or preview note.
  • Demo robot analysis or observation note with photo/sketch reference.

Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Teams should add the selected norms, all-voices plan, disagreement plan, reset/repair plan, final name, identity statement, reasons, trade-offs, evidence for how they will know the norms are working, the first sections of the Team Strategy Guide, and a note about where the Robot Behavior Specification will live. Teams should also record one design idea from Demo Robot Design Analysis to copy, one idea to modify, and one risk to avoid.

Reflection / Share-Out

  • Which norm will be hardest to keep when the robot does not work?
  • How does the team name connect to the norms and the team you want to become?

Teacher Notes

Use the two SLD handouts to keep the conversations separate: norms first, identity/name second. Use the menus to slow the conversation enough for all students to contribute. Watch for names that may not support public presentation.

The strategy guide and behavior specification should be treated as living documents. Do not create team folders unless the course later adopts a clear team-folder structure; for now, teams should copy reusable templates into their portfolio or shared team document.
