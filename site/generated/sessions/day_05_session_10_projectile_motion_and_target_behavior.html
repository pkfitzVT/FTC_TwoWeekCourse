Day 5, Session 10: Projectile Motion and Target Behavior

Purpose

Help teams connect shooter design to projectile path and goal impact.

Learning Target

Students can explain how launch angle, distance, shooter height, goal height, initial velocity, impact speed, and impact angle affect scoring.

Success Criteria

  • Teams choose a first test distance and angle.
  • Teams define what overshoot, undershoot, bounce-out, or score would mean.
  • Teams record a first shot-test plan.

Teacher Preparation

Prepare safe target area, measuring tape, target/goal, test logs, boundaries, and cleanup procedure.

Student Background Reading / Preparation

  • Projectile motion for shooters
  • Target impact and shot consistency
  • Flywheel build and test planning
  • Subsystem design cycle guide

Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: What shot path should our team test first, and what miss pattern would tell us what to change?

Work Time Options

  • Design: adjust launch angle or shooter height.
  • Testing: plan controlled shooter subsystem attempts.
  • Data analysis: define observations and success rate.
  • Documentation: shot-test table.

Must / Should / Could

  • Must: Record first shot distance, angle, power range, and expected path.
  • Should: Run or prepare at least three controlled shot trials if safe.
  • Could: Graph distance, power, angle, or success rate.

Deliverables

  • Shot-test plan.
  • Launch reasoning.
  • Testing results if testing occurs.
  • Engineering portfolio entry.

Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Teams should add shooter subsystem test result, distance, angle, power, expected path, observed miss pattern, and next change.

Reflection / Share-Out

  • Would the team rather begin with a high arc or flatter shot, and why?

Teacher Notes

Keep launch testing safe and contained. Encourage evidence over random tuning.
