Student-Led Discussion Support Coverage Matrix

Purpose

This matrix audits whether each major student-led discussion prompt has enough student-facing support for students to participate with evidence, make a decision, and document the result.

Ratings:

  • Strong: Students have prompt + background + decision/documentation support.
  • Adequate: Students have enough to proceed, but support could be clearer.
  • Partial: Some support exists, but students may need more structure.
  • Missing: Discussion exists, but no clear student-facing support document was found.
  • Unclear: More review needed.

This is an audit document only. It does not rewrite existing prompts or create missing instructional materials.

Reusable support now available: 02_student_materials/guides/how_this_robotics_course_works.md gives students a short entry-point overview of the course purpose, repeated engineering cycle, SLDs, Challenge by Choice, advanced pathways, and documentation. 02_student_materials/guides/student_led_discussion_reference_guide.md explains SLD as an agency-building decision process, including productive tone, inclusion, discussion move tallying/rubric use, SLD types, work products, growth documentation, portfolio value, and transferable collaboration skills. 02_student_materials/guides/subsystem_design_operation_testing_cycle_guide.md explains the full repeated process from game goals and robot behavior targets through design, operation, testing, tuning, acceptable performance ranges, and documentation. 90_templates/student_led_discussion_tally_sheet_template.md can be used with any 5-10 minute student-led design discussion to track participation, evidence use, decision synthesis, and growth over time. 90_templates/robot_operations_role_plan_sld_template.md and 02_student_materials/sld_prompts/reusable_robot_operations_role_plan_sld.md standardize evidence-based robot operation role planning, backups, practice rotation, and role review. 90_templates/performance_testing_postmortem_sld_template.md and 02_student_materials/sld_prompts/reusable_performance_testing_postmortem_sld.md standardize blame-free post-test diagnosis using human operation, mechanical system, software/controls, and strategy/communication categories. docs/design_choice_menu_matrix.md, 02_student_materials/activities/demo_robot_design_analysis/demo_robot_design_analysis_activity.md, and 02_student_materials/activities/demo_robot_design_analysis/demo_robot_design_observation_activity.md connect those discussions to concrete design-choice deliverables.

For a session-by-session table that pairs each official SLD prompt with a concrete deliverable, discussion type, evidence/resources, and notes/gaps, see docs/sld_prompt_deliverable_table.md.

Highest Priority Gaps

1. Mechanism architecture lacks decision-menu support. Shooter, hopper, trigger, and servo sessions have readings and test prompts, but fewer structured decision tools than chassis and controls sessions. 2. Strategy-to-behavior transition needs protected class time. Student-facing SLD sheets now exist for the Team Strategy Guide and Robot Behavior Specification, but teachers still need to protect time for both before chassis decisions. 3. Chassis decision sequencing now has a student-facing structure, but teachers still need to protect time. The Chassis Design Choice SLD sequences the menus into mini-rounds; implementation risk is rushing teams straight into building. 4. Testing artifacts could be more standardized. Shot logs, hopper jam logs, trigger position test sheets, and match/practice evidence sheets would help teams convert discussion into comparable evidence. 5. Later-session standalone SLD sheets are still limited. Day 1 Session 1, Day 2 Session 3, the Day 2 strategy/behavior bridge, Day 3 chassis design, and both Day 3 Session 6 control discussions now have standalone student-facing SLD sheets, but most later discussions still rely on teacher-facing session pages or decision menus. High-value mechanism design, testing, and presentation discussions could use student-facing SLD sheets. 6. Final presentation rehearsal support is adequate but not polished. Notebook pages and readings exist, but a rehearsal checklist or role card would make Session 19 easier to run. 7. Some engineering notebook session references are still TODO or generic. These should be resolved as new student-facing support documents are created.
