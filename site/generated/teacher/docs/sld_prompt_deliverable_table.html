Student-Led Discussion Prompt and Deliverable Table

Student-Led Discussion (SLD) is the course's decision-making process. SLD is the process. The deliverable is the recorded decision, reflection, design choice, test plan, or portfolio update that results from the discussion.

As the course progresses, SLDs shift from identity and norms, to design decisions, to reflection and improvement based on evidence.

This table treats the official docs/sessions/ pages as the session-level source for SLD prompts. It also notes student-facing supports from decision menus, readings, templates, and the engineering notebook.

Gaps and Recommendations

  • No session-level SLD prompts are missing in the official docs/sessions/ sequence. All 20 official sessions include a Student-Led Discussion / Decision prompt.
  • Several SLDs are teacher-facing only. Day 1 Session 1 has a standalone student SLD handout, but most later SLD prompts appear in teacher-facing session pages, readings, or decision menus.
  • Session 6 control decisions now have student-facing SLD handouts. Controller Mapping SLD chooses the control approach, and Drive Power / Motor Tuning SLD chooses starting power settings from evidence.
  • Autonomous/scripted behavior is present in strategy materials but not as a current official session-level SLD. The Team Strategy Guide, Robot Behavior Specification, game strategy menu, modified game rules, and engineering notebook all include autonomous planning or reflection, but the official docs/sessions/ sequence currently emphasizes trigger implementation and first shots in Session 13.
  • Mechanism decisions need stronger decision tools. Shooter/flywheel, hopper, and trigger sessions have strong readings and prompts, but lack the decision-menu structure already available for team norms, strategy, and chassis decisions.
  • Testing deliverables should be standardized. Shot logs, hopper jam logs, trigger position test sheets, whole-system test logs, and match/practice evidence sheets would help teams document evidence consistently.
  • Sentence stems and rubrics would help participation tracking. The SLD tally sheet supports tracking all voices, but later SLDs would benefit from prompt-specific stems for claim, evidence, trade-off, disagreement, revision, and synthesis.
  • Reflection after practice should be strengthened. Session 17 has a clear reliability prompt, but a match/practice evidence sheet would better connect autonomous, TeleOp, driver routine, reliability, and honest presentation claims.
  • Final presentation and portfolio reflection are clear but need polished student-facing tools. Sessions 16, 18, 19, and 20 have strong prompts; a presentation outline, rehearsal checklist, and final reflection form would make implementation more consistent.

Future Student-Facing SLD Handout Priorities

1. Shooter/flywheel design SLD focused on first variable, compression/contact plan, safety, and evidence of improvement. 2. Hopper/trigger design SLD focused on jam evidence, ball path, servo positions, and safe release/reset behavior. 3. Autonomous reflection SLD focused on which scripted behavior is reliable enough to keep and what evidence supports that decision. 4. TeleOp practice reflection SLD focused on reliability, driver routine, cycle time, failure patterns, and honest claims. 5. Final presentation/portfolio reflection SLD focused on evidence selection, balanced speaking roles, final reflection, and student agency.
