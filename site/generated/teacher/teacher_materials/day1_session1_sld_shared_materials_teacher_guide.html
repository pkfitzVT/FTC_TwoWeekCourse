Teacher / Mentor Guide

SLD 1: Shared Materials, Borrowed Equipment, and Trust

SLD Type

Community + Synthesis SLD

Purpose for Teacher / Mentor

Students should understand that robotics equipment is part of a larger community of trust. They should connect FIRST values, FTC culture, safety expectations, and borrowed equipment expectations to concrete behaviors: reporting damage, sharing limited materials, returning parts, using tools carefully, and helping other teams without taking over.

This SLD also introduces the discussion system that will be used throughout the course. It is a first chance to observe how students participate before teams are assigned.

Background Sources and Key Ideas

Source Key Ideas Students Might Use
What Is FIRST? FIRST is a global robotics community; FIRST values gracious professionalism, coopertition, teamwork, leadership, and future-ready skills.
What Is FIRST Tech Challenge? FTC teams design, build, code, compete, document, and present; FTC requires many roles and reusable materials.
Robotics Safety Norms Safety comes before the robot; announce before testing; report problems honestly; use tools correctly.
Borrowed Equipment and Community Trust Materials are borrowed or grant-funded; teams must return equipment, report damage, avoid hoarding, and protect community trust.
Orientation / robot driving experience Students may notice that robots, controllers, batteries, field space, and tools require care and coordination.

Main Prompt

What do we owe to the teams, mentors, and community members who made this robotics equipment available to us?

Follow-Up Questions

  • Why does trust matter in robotics?
  • What could damage trust between teams?
  • How is borrowed robotics equipment different from regular classroom supplies?
  • What should happen if something breaks, strips, bends, overheats, or disappears?
  • How can teams compete while still sharing materials?
  • Why should limited materials not simply be first come, first served?
  • What would it look like to help another team without taking over?

Expected Student Comments

These are not scripts. They are examples of the kinds of comments that show students are using sources, observations, reasoning, questions, and peer ideas.

Source / Quote

  • "The borrowed equipment handout says these parts come from local FTC teams."
  • "The FIRST handout says robotics is about gracious professionalism and coopertition."
  • "The safety handout says we should report broken parts honestly."
  • "The FTC handout says teams need many different roles, not just drivers or builders."
  • "The borrowed materials handout says limited materials are not first come, first served."

Observation / Experience

  • "When I saw the robot drive, I noticed it would be easy to hit something if the area was not clear."
  • "The controller and robot seemed expensive, so it makes sense that we need rules before using them."
  • "If everyone grabs parts at once, it will be hard to know where things went."
  • "When one person is driving, everyone else needs to stay aware."
  • "It looks like small parts such as screws, hubs, and hex wrenches could get lost easily."

Claim / Reasoning

  • "Trust matters because teams will not lend us materials again if we do not take care of them."
  • "Reporting damage is better than hiding it because then someone can fix the problem."
  • "Sharing materials is fairer if teams explain what they need and how they will use it."
  • "Borrowed equipment is different from regular classroom supplies because someone else needs it back."
  • "If a team takes more parts than it needs, other teams lose the chance to build and learn."

Question

  • "What happens if something breaks by accident?"
  • "How do we decide who gets a scarce part?"
  • "What should we do if two teams need the same tool or wheel?"
  • "How do we help another team without doing the work for them?"
  • "How will we know which parts are borrowed and which parts can be modified?"

Build on Peer

  • "I want to build on what ___ said about trust. If we hide damage, it affects more than just our team."
  • "Adding to ___'s point, sharing parts might slow one team down now but help the whole class succeed."
  • "I agree with ___ that reporting problems matters, and I also think we need a checkout system."
  • "What ___ said connects to coopertition because teams can compete and still help each other."

Trade-Off

  • "Sharing materials helps everyone, but it might mean one team has to wait."
  • "Testing slowly protects the robot, but it may feel less exciting at first."
  • "Letting teams request parts by plan is fairer, but it takes more time than first come, first served."
  • "Helping another team is good, but taking over could stop them from learning."

Evidence / Example

  • "If a team returns a working part, that is evidence they handled it responsibly."
  • "A materials checkout table could show who has what and when it should be returned."
  • "A photo of the robot before and after a build could help show what changed."
  • "If the portfolio records damage or repairs, then we can be honest about what happened."

Next Step / Decision

  • "We should report damaged parts right away."
  • "We should ask before modifying borrowed parts."
  • "We should have a way to request limited materials."
  • "We should return tools and parts at the end of each session."
  • "We should write thank-you notes to the teams that helped us."

Tabular Tracking Option

Student Q S O C B T E N Notes

Circle Tracking Option

Write student names around a circle. Draw arrows to show who responds to whom. Add the discussion code next to each contribution.

Example:

  • Maya -> Leo: Q
  • Leo -> Ana: C
  • Ana -> Jamal: B
  • Jamal -> Maya: T

Look for:

  • Are students responding to one another or only to the mentor?
  • Are a few students dominating?
  • Are students using sources?
  • Are students asking questions?
  • Are students naming trade-offs?
  • Are students moving toward a shared norm or next step?

Group Debrief

After the SLD, give students:

1. one group strength, 2. one next discussion goal, 3. and one connection to the next task or portfolio entry.

Example:

"Today the group made several strong claims about trust and borrowed materials. Next time, let's try to use the handouts earlier and build more directly on each other's ideas."

Portfolio / Evidence Connection

Students should record:

  • one way they will help protect borrowed equipment and community trust,
  • one idea from the discussion they want to remember,
  • and, if appropriate, one question they still have about shared materials.

Possible exit ticket:

Prompt Student Response
One way I will protect borrowed equipment is...
One discussion move I used or want to try next time is...
One question I still have is...
