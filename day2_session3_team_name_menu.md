# Team Name Menu

## What Should Our Team Name Say About Us?

## Graduate Expectation Tags

- **[Green: Personal Development]** We identify strengths, goals, and the kind of team we want to become.
- **[Purple: Curiosity & Creativity]** We create an identity that reflects our ideas and values.
- **[Blue: Effective Communication]** We explain what our name means and how it connects to our team norms.
- **[Orange: Cultural Understanding & Civic Engagement]** We represent ourselves as part of the larger robotics community.

## Decision Being Made

Your team is choosing a name. This is your first public team identity. A team name can be serious, funny, technical, creative, or values-based, but it should say something about the kind of team you want to become.

Your team name does not have to be perfect forever. It needs to be good enough to represent your team now and help you start building a shared identity.

## Background Sources

Use ideas from:

- **Why Engineering Teams Need Different Kinds of People**
- **What Is FIRST?**
- **What Is FIRST Tech Challenge?**
- your selected team norms
- your individual bios and goals
- the SLD on good robotics teammates

## Why This Decision Matters

A team name is a small decision, but it tests important team habits:

- Who gets to suggest ideas?
- Who listens?
- How does the team handle disagreement?
- Does the team choose quickly, thoughtfully, or creatively?
- Can the team make a decision everyone can support?

This is a low-risk way to practice the same decision process your team will later use for robot design, code, strategy, and presentations.

## How to Use This Menu

1. Read the name categories.
2. Choose names you like or use the categories to invent your own.
3. Discuss what each name suggests about your team.
4. Connect the name to at least two team norms.
5. Record your team name, meaning, trade-off, and evidence plan in the portfolio.

# Create Your Own Name

Your team may create its own name instead of choosing from the menu.

If you create your own name, it should:

- be school appropriate,
- be easy to say during presentations,
- connect to at least two team norms,
- suggest something about your team identity,
- and be something the whole team can support.

# SLD Connection

## Main SLD Prompt

**Should a team name describe who the team already is, or who the team wants to become?**

## Supporting Questions

- What should other teams understand when they hear our name?
- Does our name connect to our team norms?
- Does our name include everyone on the team?
- Is our name more about performance, creativity, teamwork, coding, resilience, or community?
- Can everyone support this name as good enough to try?
