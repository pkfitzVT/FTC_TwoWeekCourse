# Day 5, Session 9: Flywheel Construction Basics

## Purpose

Introduce the flywheel shooter as a system from motor power to wheel speed to ball compression to launch speed.

## Learning Target

Students can explain how motor power, wheel speed, torque, ball contact, compression, and launch speed affect a shooter.

## Success Criteria

- Teams sketch a shooter.
- Teams identify a first compression/gap plan.
- Teams define one variable to test.

## Teacher Preparation

Prepare shooter motors, wheels, hubs, mounting materials, balls, tools, batteries, safety boundaries, and stop procedures.

## Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: What first shooter variable should our team test, and what evidence will show whether it improved?

## Work Time Options

- Design: start the shooter subsystem cycle with a shooter sketch and compression plan.
- Fabrication: first mockup or mounting test.
- Testing: hand-check ball path and wheel clearance.
- Documentation: testable hypothesis.

## Must / Should / Could

- **Must:** Create a shooter subsystem sketch and identify the first compression/contact plan.
- **Should:** Build a safe first mockup or mount.
- **Could:** Compare wheel firmness, gap, or motor power as a controlled variable.

## Deliverables

- Design drawing.
- Compression plan.
- First build/mockup photo.
- Testable hypothesis.

## Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Teams should add shooter subsystem cycle entry, shooter system sketch, first variable, expected result, and safety risk.

## Reflection / Share-Out

- Why is the fastest possible wheel speed not always the best shooter design?

## Teacher Notes

Watch for unsafe spinning-wheel handling. Keep teams focused on testable prototypes rather than perfect shooters.
