# Day 1, Session 2: Collaborative Engineering Teams

## Purpose

Establish a course culture where students participate fully, rotate through different kinds of work, help each other try unfamiliar robotics tasks, and continue collecting demo robot design evidence.

## Learning Target

Students can describe how they might contribute to an engineering team and how teammates can make room for different strengths.

## Success Criteria

- Students identify several useful team contributions.
- Students distinguish helping from taking over.
- Students name one behavior that invites participation.
- Students explain how communication, timing, and role clarity affect operating a demo robot.

## Teacher Preparation

Prepare the collaborative engineering teams reading, demo robot rotation stations, the Demo Robot Design Analysis activity, and decide whether student bio and rotation information will support team formation.

## Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: How should an engineering team make room for different kinds of contributors?

## Work Time Options

- Discussion: strengths, quiet voices, and helping without taking over.
- Demo robot analysis: continue rotations with a deeper focus on chassis geometry, materials, motor placement, omni wheel placement, Control Hub and battery placement, shooter design, hopper design, trigger reliability, and operator experience.
- Partner rotation: students should operate demo robots with more than one partner when possible. This is not a ranking activity; it is a chance to practice working with different people and notice what helps a drive team function well.
- Documentation: individual contribution note.
- Team formation: create or confirm teams of 4-5 students.

## Must / Should / Could

- **Must:** Record one contribution the student can make.
- **Should:** Identify one kind of work the student wants to try and one design feature they might borrow, modify, or avoid.
- **Could:** Name a way to help a teammate learn without taking over or compare how two partner pairings changed robot operation.

## Deliverables

- Teamwork reflection or bio-based contribution note.
- Continued Demo Robot Design Analysis notes.
- Optional team formation notes.
- Engineering portfolio entry.

## Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Students should add one contribution they can make, one kind of work they want to try, one team behavior they want to practice, and demo robot design evidence to use during strategy, robot behavior, and chassis decisions.

## Reflection / Share-Out

- What is one kind of work you want to try during the course?
- What does helpful teamwork sound like?
- What partner communication helped a demo robot operate more smoothly?

## Teacher Notes

Notice leadership patterns and students who may need structured entry points. Reinforce that students are not locked into fixed roles. Demo robot rotations should help students learn about communication and design choices, not rank classmates.
