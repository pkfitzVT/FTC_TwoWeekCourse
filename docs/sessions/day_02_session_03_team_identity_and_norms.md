# Day 2, Session 3: Team Identity and Team Norms

## Purpose

Give teams a first low-risk decision-making experience before major robot design choices.

## Learning Target

Students can help choose a team name and team norms using reasons and trade-offs.

## Success Criteria

- Each team chooses a name.
- Each team chooses norms and explains why they matter.
- Teams record trade-offs and repair agreements.

## Teacher Preparation

Prepare team assignments, the team name menu, the team norms menu, and portfolio recording space.

## Student Background Reading / Preparation

- [Why team names matter](../../02_student_materials/readings/day2_session3_why_team_names_matter.md)
- [Team name menu](../../02_student_materials/decision_menus/day2_session3_team_name_menu.md)
- [Team norms menu](../../02_student_materials/decision_menus/day2_session3_team_norms_menu.md)

## Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: What should our team name and norms say about the team we want to become?

## Work Time Options

- Design: team identity, name, possible logo, motto, or color ideas.
- Documentation: name and norms decision tables.
- Team process: repair agreement for when norms are not followed.

## Must / Should / Could

- **Must:** Choose a team name and at least four team norms.
- **Should:** Explain the trade-off for each norm and create a repair agreement.
- **Could:** Connect the norms to final presentation or award/recognition goals.

## Deliverables

- SLD document.
- Team name decision table.
- Team norms table.
- Team agreement.

## Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Teams should add the final name, selected norms, reasons, trade-offs, and evidence for how they will know the norms are working.

## Reflection / Share-Out

- Which norm will be hardest to keep when the robot does not work?
- How does the team name connect to the team you want to become?

## Teacher Notes

Use the menus to slow the conversation enough for all students to contribute. Watch for names that may not support public presentation.
