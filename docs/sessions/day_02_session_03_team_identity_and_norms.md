# Day 2, Session 3: Team Identity and Team Norms

## Purpose

Give teams a first low-risk decision-making experience before major robot design choices, then connect team norms to the first game strategy document.

## Learning Target

Students can help choose a team name and team norms using reasons and trade-offs.

## Success Criteria

- Each team chooses a name.
- Each team chooses norms and explains why they matter.
- Teams record trade-offs and repair agreements.
- Teams begin a Team Strategy Guide and preview the Robot Behavior Specification that they will revisit before major design discussions.

## Teacher Preparation

Prepare team assignments, the team name menu, the team norms menu, the Team Strategy Guide template, the Robot Behavior Specification template, and portfolio recording space.

## Student-Led Discussion / Decision

Prompt: What should our team name and norms say about the team we want to become?

## Work Time Options

- Design: team identity, name, possible logo, motto, or color ideas.
- Documentation: name and norms decision tables.
- Team process: repair agreement for when norms are not followed.
- Reading/discussion: connect structure and function to the idea that robot form should follow game strategy.
- Strategy: copy the Team Strategy Guide into the team portfolio or shared document and complete the identity, norms connection, and first game-understanding sections.
- Requirements: preview the Robot Behavior Specification so students see how strategy becomes robot actions and performance goals before design choices.

## Must / Should / Could

- **Must:** Choose a team name, choose at least four team norms, and start a Team Strategy Guide.
- **Should:** Explain the trade-off for each norm, create a repair agreement, and preview the Robot Behavior Specification.
- **Could:** Watch or assign competition video observation before finalizing the first strategy.

## Deliverables

- SLD document.
- Team name decision table.
- Team norms table.
- Team agreement.
- Started Team Strategy Guide copied into the team portfolio or shared document.
- Robot Behavior Specification location or preview note.

## Engineering Portfolio Evidence

Teams should add the final name, selected norms, reasons, trade-offs, evidence for how they will know the norms are working, the first sections of the Team Strategy Guide, and a note about where the Robot Behavior Specification will live.

## Reflection / Share-Out

- Which norm will be hardest to keep when the robot does not work?
- How does the team name connect to the team you want to become?

## Teacher Notes

Use the menus to slow the conversation enough for all students to contribute. Watch for names that may not support public presentation.

The strategy guide and behavior specification should be treated as living documents. Do not create team folders unless the course later adopts a clear team-folder structure; for now, teams should copy reusable templates into their portfolio or shared team document.
