# 20-Session Scope and Sequence

This is an organizing map, not a finished lesson-plan set. Session titles and missing-material notes are based on the current repository contents and the stated two-week FTC course rhythm.

| Day | Session | Session title | Main student goal | Main teacher goal | Student-led discussion prompt | Engineering notebook deliverable | Existing materials found in repo | Missing materials |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | FIRST, FTC, safety, trust, and personal goals | Understand the course community, safety expectations, borrowed equipment responsibilities, and personal growth goals. | Establish norms for safety, community trust, discussion, and student contribution. | What do we owe to the teams, mentors, and community members who made this robotics equipment available to us? | Personal goal choice, safety/community commitment, first SLD reflection, optional student bio. | `day1_session1_first_ftc_handouts.md`; `day1_session1_what_is_first.md`; `day1_session1_what_is_first_tech_challenge.md`; `day1_session1_robotics_safety_norms.md`; `day1_session1_borrowed_equipment_expectations.md`; `day1_session1_individual_goal_menu.md`; `day1_session1_student_bio_template.md`; `day1_session1_sld_shared_materials_student.md`; `day1_session1_sld_shared_materials_teacher_guide.md`; `background_documents_print_packet.pdf` | Full timed session plan; materials/setup checklist; consolidated student packet page order. |
| 1 | 2 | Collaborative engineering teams | Identify how different strengths, roles, and communication styles support a robot team. | Prepare students for team formation and collaborative expectations. | How should an engineering team make room for different kinds of contributors? | Teamwork reflection or bio-based contribution note. | `day1_session2_collaborative_engineering_teams.md` | Full session plan; student-facing SLD prompt; teacher guide; team formation process; notebook template. |
| 2 | 3 | Team identity and team norms | Choose a team name and team norms that shape how the group will work. | Help teams practice low-risk decision-making before robot design decisions. | What should our team name and norms say about the team we want to become? | Team name decision table; team norm decision table; reflection on trade-offs. | `day2_session3_why_team_names_matter.md`; `day2_session3_team_name_menu.md`; `day2_session3_team_norms_menu.md` | Full session plan; teacher SLD guide; print packet/export; team agreement form. |
| 2 | 4 | First programming session | Connect a basic FTC TeleOp program to robot hardware names, gamepad input, and motor output. | Support first code reading, configuration-name checks, and safe programming evidence. | Why must the names in the configuration file match the names in the program exactly? | Programming notebook entry with TeleOp name, hardware names, motor ports, button mappings, one code change, and test result. | `day2_session4_programming_background.md`; `day2_session4_reading_basic_teleop.md` | Full session plan; teacher guide; programming setup checklist; safe first-test procedure; source manifests for any programming PDFs. |
| 3 | 5 | Chassis build start | Begin building or modifying the drivetrain/chassis based on team decisions. | Manage tools, parts, safety, build sequence, and space constraints. | Which design choice should we protect when the build gets difficult or slow? | Build log entry with photo/sketch, parts used, first issue noticed, and next step. | `day3_session5_chassis_physics_space_background.md`; `day2_session4_chassis_shape_menu.md`; `day2_session4_chassis_material_menu.md`; `day2_session4_wheel_selection_menu.md`; `day2_session4_motor_placement_menu.md`; `day2_session4_electronics_battery_placement_menu.md`; `day3_session5_chassis_physics_space_background.pdf`; Day 2 chassis menu PDFs may support this session. | Full session plan; tool safety mini-lesson; build checklist; notebook build-log template; materials checkout form. |
| 3 | 6 | Chassis build continuation and first movement checks | Continue chassis assembly and prepare for basic movement. | Keep teams focused on alignment, secure fastening, wiring access, and test readiness. | What evidence will show that our chassis is ready for powered testing? | Chassis readiness checklist and issue list. | Day 2 Session 4 chassis, wheel, motor, electronics materials may support this session. | Full session plan; inspection checklist; teacher troubleshooting guide; test area norms. |
| 4 | 7 | Robot control introduction | Understand controller mapping and basic driver-control choices. | Introduce human-centered control decisions and connect controls to driver intuition. | How should the controller match the driver's intuition under pressure? | Controller mapping plan or control-design reflection. | `future_human_controller_mapping_background.md`; `future_human_controller_mapping_background.pdf` | Programming lesson; controller mapping menu; FTC SDK/code examples; teacher guide; notebook code-record template. |
| 4 | 8 | Basic TeleOp programming and drivetrain test | Program or configure basic driver-controlled movement and test it safely. | Support first code deployment, troubleshooting, and safe robot testing. | What should we change first when the robot does not drive the way we expected? | TeleOp test record with observed behavior, code/config change, and result. | `future_human_controller_mapping_background.md` may support this session. | Full programming materials; step-by-step setup; troubleshooting guide; test table. |
| 5 | 9 | Strategy check and mechanism planning | Decide what additional mechanism or scoring feature is realistic for the remaining time. | Keep teams aligned to time, materials, game strategy, and reliability. | What is the simplest mechanism that could improve our strategy without risking the whole robot? | Mechanism decision table, sketch, and build-risk note. | `day2_session4_game_strategy_goals_menu.md`; `day2_session4_decode_scoring_strategy_background.md` may support this session. | Mechanism design menus; background readings; session plan; teacher guide; notebook mechanism template. |
| 5 | 10 | Mechanism build or robot improvement sprint | Build, attach, or improve the selected mechanism or drivetrain feature. | Guide teams toward testable increments and safe modifications. | What should count as good enough to test today? | Build sprint log with testable goal, evidence photo/sketch, and unresolved problem. | No dedicated mechanism-build material found. | Full session plan; mechanism build checklist; tool/material guide; failure/iteration log. |
| 6 | 11 | Testing methods and data collection | Learn how to test one change at a time and collect useful evidence. | Establish repeatable testing routines and data expectations. | What evidence is more useful than "it worked" or "it did not work"? | Test plan and first data table. | Team norms menu includes "Test One Change at a Time"; design menus include evidence prompts. | Testing background reading; test-plan template; teacher guide; sample data table. |
| 6 | 12 | Iterate from test evidence | Use test data to decide what to adjust next. | Help teams connect evidence to design changes instead of random tinkering. | When should a team fix, simplify, or abandon an idea? | Iteration record: problem, evidence, change, result. | Design menu files include decision/reflection structures. | Full session plan; iteration protocol; teacher troubleshooting notes; reflection prompt. |
| 7 | 13 | Autonomous or scripted robot behavior | Add a basic autonomous or repeatable programmed action appropriate to the course constraints. | Scope autonomous goals realistically and support code testing. | What autonomous goal is reliable enough to be worth keeping? | Autonomous plan and test results. | `day2_session4_game_strategy_goals_menu.md` includes autonomous strategy options; `day2_session4_decode_scoring_strategy_background.md` includes autonomous goals. | Programming lesson; code examples; autonomous test template; teacher guide. |
| 7 | 14 | Driver practice and reliability tuning | Practice driving, tune controls, and identify reliability problems. | Set practice structure and help teams separate driver skill from robot design issues. | Is this problem caused by the robot, the code, the driver, or the strategy? | Driver practice log with observed issue and next adjustment. | `future_human_controller_mapping_background.md`; game strategy menu may support this session. | Driver practice drills; reliability checklist; field setup notes; notebook log. |
| 8 | 15 | Documentation and engineering portfolio work session | Organize evidence into an engineering notebook/portfolio. | Ensure every team has usable documentation of decisions, tests, and trade-offs. | What evidence best explains our team's engineering story? | Portfolio evidence checklist and missing-evidence list. | Menus include portfolio deliverables; SLD materials include evidence/reflection structures. | Consolidated engineering notebook; portfolio checklist; teacher review guide; example structure. |
| 8 | 16 | Final robot improvements and test round | Make final design/code adjustments based on evidence and presentation needs. | Limit scope and keep teams focused on reliable improvements. | What final change is worth the risk, and what should we leave alone? | Final improvement decision record and final test evidence. | Existing design menus can support trade-off language. | Full session plan; final test checklist; scope-control guide. |
| 9 | 17 | Match/practice challenge and evidence collection | Run practice matches or challenge attempts and collect final performance evidence. | Coordinate field use, safety, scoring, and evidence capture. | What does our robot do reliably, and what should we be honest about? | Match/practice data table, photo/video evidence note, and reliability reflection. | `day2_session4_decode_scoring_strategy_background.md`; `day2_session4_game_strategy_goals_menu.md` | Match rules packet; scoring sheet; teacher field-management guide; media/evidence checklist. |
| 9 | 18 | Presentation planning | Plan final presentation around robot function, decisions, evidence, trade-offs, and learning. | Help teams turn notebook evidence into a clear final explanation. | What story should our team tell about what we built, tested, changed, and learned? | Presentation outline tied to notebook evidence. | FTC orientation files mention presentation/portfolio roles; menus include decision evidence. | Presentation template; rubric; student planning sheet; teacher conference guide. |
| 10 | 19 | Final presentation preparation and rehearsal | Rehearse final explanation and improve clarity. | Coach concise evidence-based presentation and equitable team participation. | How can every teammate contribute to explaining the robot and the process? | Rehearsal feedback notes and final revision list. | No dedicated final-presentation material found. | Rehearsal protocol; feedback form; final checklist; teacher guide. |
| 10 | 20 | Final demonstrations, presentations, and reflection | Demonstrate the robot, present the engineering story, and reflect on growth. | Facilitate final demonstrations, collect evidence, and close the course. | What did we learn about building, programming, testing, documenting, and working as a team? | Final reflection and completed notebook/portfolio submission. | `day1_session1_individual_goal_menu.md` includes final reflection preview. | Final reflection template; presentation rubric; demonstration scoring sheet; course closure materials. |
